# Trim DSA scope, keep it in the plan

DSA is in about half of 2026 frontend loops, but it makes up only about one round in eight. The evidence covers November 2025 to November 2026. After removing loops that appear in more than one notes file, I counted **28 first-hand frontend loops where DSA status is known; 13 (46%) included some DSA**. In round-level tallies, **DSA was the main content of only 11–13% of rounds**. It appeared as part of a mixed round in about 25%. JavaScript fundamentals and machine coding each took about 30% of rounds, and frontend system design appeared in every senior loop. Where difficulty was stated, it was **mostly LeetCode Easy–Medium on arrays, strings, sliding window and tree/graph BFS/DFS**. One DP Hard (Burst Balloons) did sink a UiPath SDE-2 candidate. Big tech and global product companies (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Uber, Adobe, Harness, UiPath) almost always kept a DSA round. Indian consumer and fintech companies (Razorpay, Zepto, Groww, CRED, Swiggy SDE-2, Meesho, Jio Hotstar) mostly did not. For a 3-YOE frontend developer in India, the current plan gives DSA 2.5 of 8 hours a day (about 36% of study time), and Namaste DSA covers the full 23-section general-SWE syllabus. That is **a moderate over-investment in hours and a large one in scope**. The fix is to cut DSA to about 1.5–2 hours a day, finish only the frontend-relevant Namaste sections, and move the freed time to machine coding. The sample is small and skewed: about 10 full-text posts, roughly 5 independent Medium authors, no readable Reddit threads, and most LeetCode and Blind content seen only as search snippets. Treat every percentage below as directional.

## Four of every five rounds test frontend skills, not algorithms

The notes include two independent round-level tallies, built with different methods. They cannot be pooled directly, but they point the same way. The Medium tally read 10 free posts in full through RSS, covering 36 rounds. It counts a round under every type it contained. The non-Medium tally used LeetCode Discuss, GeeksforGeeks and frontend-junction, covering 9 classifiable loops and 23 rounds, and gives each round one primary type. No loop appears in both tallies. The overlapping Microsoft loop was counted only on the non-Medium side, because on Medium it was a paywalled post outside the full-text set.

|---|---|---|---|

The synthesis column is my estimate, not a measured figure. The pattern is clear even so. **Every Medium full-text loop (10/10) had a JS or machine-coding round, and 8/10 had frontend system design**, while only 4 of 36 rounds were pure DSA ([UiPath](https://medium.com/@vaibhav11t/i-interviewed-at-uipath-frontend-sde-2-react-machine-coding-lld-dsa-one-brutal-reality-check-cbd08859e99d), [Harness](https://medium.com/@yashpreetbathla/harness-senior-software-engineer-frontend-interview-experience-all-8-rounds-a06f8347dda5), [Adobe](https://medium.com/@yashpreetbathla/adobe-frontend-interview-experience-all-4-rounds-questions-how-i-cr)). On the other platforms, **7 of 9 loops included a machine-coding or take-home UI build**, while about 4 of 9 had a dedicated DSA round ([GfG EZ Works](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/interview-experiences/ez-works-frontend-developer-interview-experience-rounds-1-2/), [LeetCode Hotstar](https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/7598082/), [LeetCode Headout](https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/7792155/)).

DSA also turns up inside rounds that look like JavaScript rounds. Kotak's "Bar Raiser" round put a 15-minute LC 3 Longest Substring inside a JS-internals discussion, and the final design round ended with a "short DSA problem" ([Kotak](https://medium.com/@anjaliraheja1850/kotak-mahindra-bank-senior-frontend-engineer-interview-experience-fa350e29c85e)). APIwiz's SDE-1 technical round included array-flatten, missing-number and string-compression problems among React debugging tasks ([GfG APIwiz](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/interview-experiences/apiwiz-sde-1-frontend/)). Pure DSA time is therefore small, but some DSA fluency is needed in more loops than the "dedicated DSA round" count suggests.

**How I deduplicated.** Several loops appear in more than one notes file. The Microsoft 2026 loop on frontend-junction is the same as the Frontend Army Medium post. The Google L4 2026 loop may be. The LeetCode Jio Hotstar SDE-2 post and the Frontend Army "JioHotstar 48 LPA" post describe the same two-month rejection. Razorpay has four overlapping write-ups (Mocklingo May 2026, two Frontend Army posts, TechTrends Jul 2026). Headout has two posts with different seniority and dates: a Medium post from a senior engineer and a LeetCode post from a 10-YOE lead. Both are counted once as a single "no DSA" company data point. I also **excluded the Postman post**: it was published Sep 2026, but the author applied in Sep 2024 ([Postman](https://saumyadip25.medium.com/postman-senior-engineer-frontend-interview-experience-feee8e512d9d)). Without it, the Medium figure for loops with any DSA falls from 5/10 to 4/9.

## Medium problems on arrays, strings and trees cover most of what was asked

Across the in-window posts that named problems, the asks clustered tightly. **Sliding window and two pointers**: LC 3 at Kotak, is-subsequence at Harness, two-pointer at PhonePe. **Array and prefix-sum work**: Harness, APIwiz. **Tree and graph traversal**: LC 207 Course Schedule at UiPath, graphs at Google and Adobe, DOM BFS/DFS on a whiteboard at Harness. **Greedy**: LC 134 Gas Station at Harness. Only one problem was DP, **LC 312 Burst Balloons (Hard)**, and it was the main reason the UiPath candidate was rejected. Afterwards the candidate named "DP and graph fundamentals" as the weakness ([UiPath](https://medium.com/@vaibhav11t/i-interviewed-at-uipath-frontend-sde-2-react-machine-coding-lld-dsa-one-brutal-reality-check-cbd08859e99d)). Graph BFS/DFS appeared more often than the frontend guides suggest. The Postman post (interview in 2024) asked Number of Islands ([Postman](https://saumyadip25.medium.com/postman-senior-engineer-frontend-interview-experience-feee8e512d9d)), and an older, undated Uber frontend loop asked a graph-plus-async JS question ([LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/7322609/)). Linked lists appeared only in the undated Nykaa post. Heaps and tries did not appear at all.

Curated guidance agrees. The Frontend Interview Handbook (updated Oct 2026) names arrays, maps and sets as the core structures and **trees as the priority "because the DOM is a tree"**. It says companies "tend to go easy" on frontend candidates ([Frontend Interview Handbook](https://www.frontendinterviewhandbook.com/algorithms)). GeeksforGeeks ranks arrays, sorting/searching and hashing as high priority, and graphs, DP and advanced trees as mid- or senior-level differentiators ([GeeksforGeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/?p=1040956)). Meta's frontend coding tags split roughly **26% easy, 60% medium, 14% hard, with no DP**, framed around DOM and component trees ([SpaceComplexity Meta](https://spacecomplexity.ai/blog/meta-frontend-engineer-interview)). Walmart's and PhonePe's rounds were easy–medium. Kotak gave about 15 minutes for one Medium and expected the sliding-window solution, not brute force. The working bar for a 3-YOE India candidate is therefore **fast, clean LeetCode Mediums in JavaScript on arrays, strings, hashing, stacks and trees, plus BFS/DFS on grids and graphs**. Hards and DP matter only for Google, and possibly Amazon, Uber and UiPath-style global product loops.

The non-DSA "coding" content is just as predictable. The same utilities recur across Google, Meta, Uber, Zepto, Groww, Adobe and Flipkart: debounce, throttle, `Promise.all` and concurrency-limited task runners (Uber's `mapAsyncLimit`), event emitters, infinite currying, flatten, and event-loop output ordering ([Uber](https://medium.com/@anjaliraheja1850/uber-senior-frontend-engineer-interview-experience-2026-ddc9dd742dbd), [Zepto](https://www.frontend-junction.com/interview-experience/zepto-sde2-frontend-interview), [D. E. Shaw](https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/7664645/)). Machine coding is now graded on production concerns. Swiggy's SDE-2 rejection cited error boundaries and API fallback states, not a broken UI ([TechTrends](https://medium.com/techtrends-digest/deconstructing-the-swiggy-frontend-sde-2-interview-loop-ba3cff71d2ac)).

## Cut DSA to about 22% of study time and stop Namaste DSA after trees

None of the accessible expert sources publishes a percentage split. Scrimba's 4-week plan (Jul 2026) has **no dedicated DSA week**: JS fundamentals, timed component builds, React plus system design, then mocks and behavioral. It says LeetCode is asked "mostly at larger firms" ([Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/frontend-interview-prep-guide-2026/)). GreatFrontEnd describes the industry as "gradually shifting away" from DSA for frontend candidates ([GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/front-end-interview-guidebook/algorithms)). The split below is therefore my synthesis. It is based on the round shares above, adjusted for two facts. First, DSA acts as a **gate** where it exists: one weak round ended an otherwise passing UiPath loop. Second, machine coding is the near-universal filter.

| Area (8-hour day: ~7 h study + 1 h job hunt) | Current plan | Recommended default | Before a Google / Amazon / Microsoft / Uber / Adobe loop |
|---|---|---|---|
| DSA (Namaste + LeetCode, in JS) | 2.5 h (~36%) | **1.5 h (~22%)** | 2.5–3 h (~35–40%) |
| Machine coding (React + vanilla JS, timed) | ~1–1.5 h shared with polyfills | **2 h (~29%)** | 1.5 h |
| JS fundamentals, polyfills, output questions, browser/web | ~0.5–1 h shared | **1.5 h (~21%)** | 1 h |
| Frontend system design (RADIO) | 1.5 h shared with theory | **1 h (~14%)** | 0.75 h |
| Behavioral, mocks, revision | 1 h | **1 h (~14%)** | 0.75 h |

On the over-investment question: **in hours the current plan is moderately DSA-heavy. In scope, finishing all 23 Namaste sections would be a clear over-investment.** About 36% of study time on DSA is well above the ~12–15% of interview time it occupies and the ~50% of loops that include it. That gap only makes sense if the target list leans toward big tech. Cutting DSA to zero would be the opposite mistake, because it would close off about half of loops, including most of the well-paid global-product offices in Bengaluru. The practical move is to shift about an hour a day from DSA into machine coding and JS. Then, once a specific DSA-heavy interview is booked, raise DSA back to about 40% for the 10–14 days before it. Ask every recruiter whether the loop has an algorithms round. This is the one piece of advice every prep source agrees on.

For Namaste DSA, the course's 23 sections are a general-SWE syllabus ([NamasteDev](https://namastedev.com/learn/namaste-dsa); section list from a [third-party mirror](https://unlockedcodingsite.vercel.app/teacher/akshay-saini/Namaste-DSA), since the official page returned 403). The repo shows Warm Up, Arrays, basic Recursion, Searching/Sorting and part of Two Pointers already done. The current planned order is Linked List → Strings → Stack → Two Pointers/Sliding Window → Binary Tree.

| Priority | Namaste sections | Why (2026 evidence) | Suggested depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Must do now** | Strings; Two Pointers & Sliding Window (finish); Stack & Queues; Binary Tree | LC 3 at Kotak, two-pointer at PhonePe, is-subsequence at Harness; DOM-as-tree is the handbook's top priority; DOM BFS/DFS at Harness | Full section, re-solve Mediums timed (≤25 min) |
| **Must do, already mostly done** | Arrays, Recursion, Time & Space Complexity | Prefix-sum/array asks at Harness and APIwiz; complexity follow-ups at PhonePe | Revision only |
| **Light, cap at 1–2 days each** | Linked List; Binary Search; BST; Graphs (BFS/DFS on grids, topological sort only); Greedy (classic few) | Linked list seen only at undated Nykaa; Course Schedule (UiPath), graphs (Google, Adobe), Gas Station (Harness) | Core patterns only: reverse/merge/cycle; Islands, Course Schedule, rotated-array search |
| **Defer until a big-tech loop is booked** | DP, Heap/Priority Queue, Backtracking beyond subsets/permutations, Tries | Only one DP problem in window (UiPath Hard); no heap/trie asks seen | Skip for now |
| **Skip** | Advanced revisits (Searching/Sorting, Arrays, Strings 2nd pass); Bonus | No added interview signal | Skip |

Linked List was started on 17 Nov. Since it showed up in the evidence only once, and that post is undated, reordering is the most useful change: **wrap up Linked List quickly (reverse, merge two sorted, cycle detection, middle node), then go straight to Strings → Stack → Binary Tree**. Use the hour saved each day on a timed vanilla-JS or React build. A two-hour machine-coding round was the most common single round type in the whole sample.

## What the sample cannot tell you

These numbers come from a small, skewed corpus. The Medium full-text set has 10 loops, and **4 of the 10 come from one author and 3 from another**, so there are about five independent voices. Most posts from the largest 2026 source, the Frontend Army publication (about three posts a week), are member-only. Their round details came only from search summaries, and those summaries may omit rounds, so "DSA not mentioned" there does not prove there was none. Medium article pages returned HTTP 403, and only RSS feeds were readable. LeetCode Discuss returned 403 and Blind 402, so those loops are snippet-based. **No Reddit thread surfaced in any search**, Glassdoor and AmbitionBox gave little round-level data, and namastedev.com and GreatFrontEnd were blocked. No in-window post stated exactly 3 YOE in India with full rounds, and most YOE figures are inferred from titles (SDE-1/SDE-2/Senior). The corpus also over-represents senior roles, India-based authors and notable loops. The company table mixes first-hand posts with aggregator guides (SpaceComplexity, intervue.io, Prepfully), which the notes flagged as lower reliability. Treat a 46% versus 56% difference in "loops with DSA" as noise. The robust findings are the direction and the company-type pattern, not the exact figures.

## Conclusion

The data does not say frontend developers should skip DSA. It says DSA is a **gate in some loops rather than the main subject of frontend interviews**, and the gates sit in predictable places: big tech, global-product India offices, and a few Indian fintechs and e-commerce companies. That makes DSA prep a targeting decision as much as a study decision. A candidate who applies first to Razorpay, Zepto, Groww, CRED, Swiggy SDE-2 and similar companies can interview while still working on DSA, as long as their machine coding and JS are sharp. The same candidate should hold Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Uber, Adobe, Atlassian and Walmart until Strings, Stack, Trees and basic graphs feel automatic.

DSA time also pays off beyond the algorithms round. The DSA that counted in 2026 was the kind that overlaps with frontend work: tree traversal that is really DOM traversal, sliding window under a 15-minute limit, and BFS that doubles as a `getElementsByClassName` polyfill. Practicing those problems in JavaScript, aloud and under a timer, prepares for the DSA round and the JS round together. Grinding DP and tries prepares for neither at most of the companies a 3-YOE frontend developer in India is likely to interview with first.
